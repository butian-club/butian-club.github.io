From **13 to 17 August 2026**, the Global Future Space Scholars Meet (GFSSM) brought young participants to Beijing.
Butian Engineering Club from Hangzhou No.2 High School entered **Teams Tianji and Tianshu**; both advanced from the qualifying round to the national final.
Their task was to imagine a mining settlement in the asteroid belt in the year 2115.

## The scenario: a city on Psyche in 2115

The challenge called for a space city serving mining operations on the M-type asteroid **Psyche**.
The teams had to connect infrastructure, habitat, structure, operations and a business case in a proposal they could defend together.

### In qualification: Tianshu's engineering baseline

For the qualifying round, Tianshu developed an original model with a **non-rotating central axis, one torus and four spokes**.
Its reviewed engineering baseline specifies a 640 m outer diameter, a 480 m inner diameter and a target range of 0.75g to 1g across the torus.
These figures show how the team turned a vision of living in space into a structure they could calculate and review.

## From practice briefs to the 24-hour challenge

Before the final, new members learned unfamiliar software, worked with complex data and completed practice tasks under the guidance of older club members.
They then drafted and revised qualifying proposals. At the final, they joined virtual companies, ran for leadership roles, rehearsed their defenses and worked with students from other teams.

The **24-hour challenge began at 8 a.m. on 15 August**. Company members designed a surface base on Psyche while negotiating early communication problems,
rebuilding parts of their proposals, reaching agreement late at night and checking the final work before dawn.
Afterward, more than 40 company members met to recognize one another's contributions and identify what to improve.

## Experience passed between cohorts

**Zhu Maoqi** advised the teams. **Hu Yue**, a former Butian Tianji competitor from 2021, returned as an academic adviser to answer questions and help with the review.
Former club president **Qian Kun** and former vice president **Shen Yue** were among the older members supporting this year's participants.

In their reflections, Sun Boqiao and Fang Shengyi both described the work beyond the technical design: resolving conflicting views,
coordinating across teams and languages, and weighing organizational and commercial decisions.
Those lessons now sit alongside the results as material for the next cohort.
